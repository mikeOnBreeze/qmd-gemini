# Startup Fundraising Guide

## Overview

Raising money for a startup is one of the most challenging and time-consuming parts of building a company. This guide covers the fundraising lifecycle from pre-seed through Series A and beyond.

## Funding Stages

### Pre-Seed ($50K–$500K)
This is typically friends and family money, or small angel checks. You need a compelling idea, maybe a prototype, and a credible founding team. The goal is to get enough capital to build an MVP and validate initial demand.

### Seed ($500K–$3M)
Seed rounds come from angel investors and early-stage VCs. You should have an MVP, early traction (users, revenue, or engagement metrics), and a clear thesis on how you'll grow. Typical dilution is 15-25%.

### Series A ($5M–$20M)
Series A fundraising is where things get serious. You need proven product-market fit, meaningful revenue or growth metrics, and a clear path to scale. This is where the big venture capital firms come in — Sequoia, a16z, Benchmark, Accel.

## Preparing Your Pitch

### The Sequoia Investor Pitch Format

Sequoia Capital popularized a pitch structure that most VCs now expect:

1. **Company purpose** — One sentence describing what you do
2. **Problem** — What pain point exists today
3. **Solution** — How you solve it
4. **Why now** — What's changed to make this possible
5. **Market size** — TAM, SAM, SOM
6. **Product** — Demo or screenshots
7. **Business model** — How you make money
8. **Team** — Why you're the right people
9. **Financials** — Revenue, growth rate, projections
10. **The ask** — How much you're raising and what you'll do with it

### Key Metrics to Have Ready

- Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) and growth rate
- Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)
- Lifetime Value (LTV)
- Burn rate and runway
- Net Revenue Retention (NRR)

## Managing Runway

Your **runway** is how many months you can operate before running out of money. Calculate it as:

```
Runway (months) = Cash in bank / Monthly burn rate
```

A healthy startup should maintain at least 12-18 months of runway. When you drop below 6 months, it's time to either raise again or cut costs aggressively.

### Cash Flow Tips

- Monitor burn rate weekly, not monthly
- Build financial models with pessimistic, realistic, and optimistic scenarios
- Keep 3-6 months of operating costs as a buffer
- Don't count receivables as cash — they're not liquid until collected
- Plan your next raise when you still have 9+ months of runway

## Term Sheet Basics

Key terms to understand when negotiating:

- **Pre-money valuation** — Company value before the investment
- **Post-money valuation** — Pre-money + investment amount
- **Liquidation preference** — Who gets paid first in an exit
- **Anti-dilution** — Protection against future down rounds
- **Board seats** — How many directors and who appoints them
- **Pro-rata rights** — Right to maintain ownership percentage in future rounds

## Common Mistakes

1. Raising too little — You'll be fundraising again in 6 months
2. Raising too much — Overinflated valuation creates pressure
3. Ignoring unit economics — Revenue without margins isn't a business
4. Not building relationships early — Start talking to investors 6-12 months before you need money
5. Spending before closing — Don't hire until the wire hits your account
